Flock AI Camera Information and Resources

https://i.imgur.com/q54nis0.jpeg

AI-driven cameras have been popping up all around cities are made by Flock Safety, a private company. These cameras record information from every passing vehicle and person and store the following information: - 7 to 12 photos of every car - License plate information - Make, model, and color of your car - Bumper stickers - Any dents/damage to your car - Facial recognition data - Race - Estimated age - Clothing type - And more… This information is stored in a national database that can be accessed by law enforcement such as local police. However, this data can also be accessed by any other law enforcement agency that has access to the database. This includes agencies like ICE who actively use the cameras to track and deport people without a warrant or probable cause. Additionally, Flock camera data can be accessed by private individuals or companies that have a contract with Flock Safety. There have already been multiple instances across the US of Flock camera data being abused by law enforcement, private individuals, and Flock Safety’s own employees. These cameras can be used to create profiles of any individual or vehicle that is photographed. This information has led to unlawful arrests, assault, stalking, and harassment. While Flock Safety claims that they do not use the cameras to actively track vehicles or people around a city or town, law enforcement and government agencies can use the data gathered to create real-time maps of where you and your vehicle have been by combining data from multiple cameras around town. At CalArts, we value social, artistic, and cultural freedom above all else. These cameras and the general rise of indiscriminate surveillance across the US are actively encroaching on those values. Already, cities across the country have ended their contracts with Flock Safety because of these concerns. We must stay vigilant and proactive when it comes to mass surveillance so we can protect the values that we as a community and a society hold dear.
